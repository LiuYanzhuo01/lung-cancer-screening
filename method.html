Methodology

Multimodal Deep Learning Pipeline for PET/CT Analysis

Overview Method Results & Impact
PET/CT Input → Preprocessing & Registration → Multimodal Feature Extraction → Deep Learning Fusion → Risk Stratification
1

Data Acquisition & Preprocessing

Paired PET and CT volumes are collected and spatially registered. CT images provide high-resolution anatomical context, while PET images supply metabolic activity maps. Standardized uptake value (SUV) normalization and intensity windowing are applied to reduce inter-scan variability.

2

Multimodal Feature Extraction

Separate feature encoders process CT and PET channels. CT-focused layers capture nodule morphology (shape, margin, texture), while PET-focused layers capture metabolic heterogeneity and intensity distribution. Early-stage lesions often show subtle functional changes that may precede clear structural abnormalities.

3

Deep Learning Fusion Strategy

Extracted features are fused at an intermediate layer using attention-guided concatenation. The model learns to weight PET and CT contributions adaptively depending on lesion characteristics. This allows the system to leverage metabolic signals when anatomical findings are ambiguous, and vice versa.

4

Classification & Risk Scoring

The fused representation is passed through fully connected layers to output a malignancy risk score. The system is designed to prioritize high sensitivity at the early screening stage, where missing a true positive carries significant clinical consequences.

5

Evaluation Framework

Model performance is assessed using sensitivity, specificity, AUC-ROC, and false positive rate at clinically relevant operating points. Subgroup analysis by nodule size and stage helps evaluate whether the multimodal approach offers particular benefit for the earliest detectable lesions.
